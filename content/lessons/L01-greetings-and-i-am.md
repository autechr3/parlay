# Lesson 01 — Greetings & "I am"

**Goal:** By the end of this hour you can greet someone, say your name, say your nationality and job, ask how someone is, and say goodbye — all in Farsi script.

---

## 0. Warm-up (3 min)

Nothing to review yet. Instead, three facts that make Persian easier than you expect:

1. **No grammatical gender.** No der/die/das, no el/la. None.
2. **No articles.** No "a," no "the." کتاب can mean "book," "a book," or "the book."
3. **One word for he and she.** او (u) covers both. Persian does not gender its pronouns.

And one that makes it harder: **the verb goes at the end.** Persian is Subject–Object–Verb. "I am American" is literally *I American am*.

---

## 1. Grammar (12 min)

### 1a. Subject pronouns

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| من | man | I |
| تو | to | you *(singular, informal — friends, family, kids)* |
| او | u | he / she *(people)* |
| آن | ân | it / that *(things)* |
| ما | mâ | we |
| شما | shomâ | you *(plural, OR singular formal — strangers, elders, anyone you're being polite to)* |
| آنها | ânhâ | they |

> **Use شما with Negar's parents. Use تو with Negar.** Getting this wrong is the single most noticeable politeness error a learner makes. When in doubt, use شما — being over-formal is a mild oddity; being under-formal is rude.

### 1b. "To be" — the full form

The verb **بودن** (budan, "to be") has a standalone present form built on **هست‑**:

| Person | Farsi | Translit |
|---|---|---|
| I am | هستم | hastam |
| you are *(sg)* | هستی | hasti |
| he/she is | است | ast |
| we are | هستیم | hastim |
| you are *(pl)* | هستید | hastid |
| they are | هستند | hastand |

Notice those endings: **‑م، ‑ی، ‑د، ‑یم، ‑ید، ‑ند**. Memorize them now. They are the personal endings for *every verb in the language*, in every tense. You are learning them once.

The 3rd-person singular is the odd one — **است** (ast), not هستد.

**Sentence pattern:**
> من آمریکایی **هستم**. — man âmrikâyi **hastam**. — I am American.
> او ایرانی **است**. — u irâni **ast**. — She is Iranian.
> ما معلم **هستیم**. — mâ mo'allem **hastim**. — We are teachers.

Note: ما معلم هستیم — the noun stays **singular**. Persian does not say "we are teachers."

### 1c. "To be" — the short (enclitic) form

In practice, Persian usually glues a shortened copula onto the end of the previous word. After a word ending in a consonant:

| | Full | Enclitic |
|---|---|---|
| I am good | من خوب هستم | من خوب**م** — khub**am** |
| you are good | تو خوب هستی | تو خوب**ی** — khub**i** |
| he/she is good | او خوب است | او خوب**ه**\* — khub**e** |
| we are good | ما خوب هستیم | ما خوب**یم** — khub**im** |
| you are good | شما خوب هستید | شما خوب**ید** — khub**id** |
| they are good | آنها خوب هستند | آنها خوب**ند** — khub**and** |

\* The ‑ه form is *spoken*. In formal writing you keep است. See §5.

**Both forms are correct.** Use هستم-forms for now — they're clearer and always safe. The enclitic will become automatic through exposure.

## 2. Vocabulary (8 min)

### Greetings

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| سلام | salâm | hello *(any time, any register)* |
| صبح بخیر | sobh bekheyr | good morning |
| شب بخیر | shab bekheyr | good night |
| خداحافظ | khodâhâfez | goodbye *(lit. "God protect you")* |
| به سلامت | be salâmat | go safely *(said to the one leaving)* |
| ممنون | mamnun | thank you |
| خواهش می‌کنم | khâhesh mikonam | you're welcome / please |
| بله | bale | yes |
| نه | na | no |

### People & identity

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| اسم | esm | name |
| آقا | âqâ | Mr. / sir |
| خانم | khânom | Ms. / ma'am |
| آمریکایی | âmrikâyi | American |
| ایرانی | irâni | Iranian |
| معلم | mo'allem | teacher |
| مهندس | mohandes | engineer |
| دانشجو | dâneshju | university student |
| خوب | khub | good, well |

### Two fixed phrases — memorize whole, don't analyze

> **حال شما چطور است؟** — hâl-e shomâ chetor ast? — How are you? *(formal)*
> **اسم من مت است.** — esm-e man Matt ast. — My name is Matt.

That little **‑e** in *hâl-**e**  shomâ* and *esm-**e** man* is the **ezâfe**, the most important sound in Persian. It isn't written. You'll learn it properly in Lesson 03. For now: hear it, copy it, don't worry about it.

### Pronunciation notes

- **â** = the "a" in *father*, but rounder and further back — closer to British "hot." **a** = the "a" in *cat*. These are different letters in Persian and confusing them changes words.
- **kh** (خ) = the *ch* in Bach. **q** (ق/غ) = a *k* made at the very back of the throat.
- **Stress**: on the **last** syllable of nouns and adjectives — âmrikâ**yi**, mo'al**lem**, sa**lâm**.

## 4. Production — roleplay (7 min)

You meet Negar's uncle for the first time. Write out the exchange in script, then read it aloud.

> **You:** greet him, formally
> **Him:** سلام. حال شما چطور است؟
> **You:** say you're well, thank him, ask him back
> **Him:** ممنون، من هم خوب هستم. اسم شما چیست؟
> **You:** give your name, say you're American, say you're an engineer
> **Him:** خوشوقتم.
> **You:** say goodbye

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

Everything above is correct formal Persian. Here is what you'll actually hear in her house.

| Formal (what you learned) | Colloquial (what she says) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| حال شما چطور است؟ | حال شما چطوره؟ / حالتون چطوره؟ | ast → ‑e, always |
| تو چطور هستی؟ | چطوری؟ | the everyday "how are you" |
| — | سلام، خوبی؟ | the *most* common casual greeting, full stop |
| من خوب هستم | خوبم | |
| او ایرانی است | اون ایرانیه | **آن → اون** (ân → un) |
| آنها | اونا | ânhâ → unâ |
| ممنون | مرسی / ممنون | مرسی (French *merci*) is extremely common |
| خداحافظ | خدافظ | khodâhâfez → khodâfez |
| اسم من مت است | اسمم مته | esm-am Matt-e |

**The single rule that unlocks half of colloquial Persian:** written **ân** becomes spoken **un**. آن→اون، نان→نون (bread), خانه→خونه (house), می‌خوانم→می‌خونم. It is that consistent.

**Cultural note — تعارف (ta'ârof):** When you say ممنون, the ritual answer is خواهش می‌کنم (khâhesh mikonam). If someone offers you something, a first refusal is often expected politeness rather than a real "no." You will encounter this constantly. For now just know the word exists and watch how Negar's family does it.

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 4 minutes. Give her this box as-is.**

> Matt is going to say six sentences. Please listen and tell him:
> 1. Did he distinguish **â** (as in سلام) from **a** (as in بد)? This is the #1 American-accent error.
> 2. Is his stress on the **last** syllable of each word?
> 3. Does his خ sound like a real خ, or like an English "h" or "k"?
>
> Don't correct grammar — just sound. Then say each sentence back to him the way you'd naturally say it, so he hears the colloquial version.
>
> **His six sentences:**
> ۱. سلام، حال شما چطور است؟
> ۲. من خوب هستم، ممنون.
> ۳. اسم من مت است.
> ۴. من آمریکایی هستم.
> ۵. من مهندس هستم.
> ۶. خداحافظ.

## 7. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Writing (8 min).** Write the six forms of بودن with the adjective خسته, in script, three times each. Then write six sentences of your own using a different subject pronoun in each.

**H2 — Translation (5 min).** Into Farsi script:
1. Hello, how are you?
2. I am good, thank you.
3. My name is Matt.
4. He is a teacher.
5. We are American.
6. Goodbye.

**H3 — Self-introduction (4 min).** Write a 4-sentence self-introduction in script. Name, nationality, job, and one adjective about how you are today. Read it aloud three times.

**H4 — Colloquial conversion (3 min).** Rewrite these three in the colloquial form:
1. او ایرانی است.
2. من خوب هستم.
3. آنها آمریکایی هستند.

**Log it:** vocabulary items 1–18 into your review deck. Mark today's lesson complete.

## Tutor notes

- Do not let the student write transliteration instead of script. If a letter isn't in their workbook yet, they write it anyway and you correct the shape.
- The two ezâfe phrases in §2 are deliberately unexplained. If the student asks, confirm it's real and coming in Lesson 03. Don't teach it early — it needs its own hour.
- Common error to watch for: putting the verb in second position, English-style. Flag it every single time in Unit 1; it's the habit that decides whether SOV becomes automatic.
- Second common error: using تو with a stranger. Correct it warmly but always.
- If the student finishes early, extend §4 by having them introduce a third person (او) rather than themselves.
