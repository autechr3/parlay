# Lesson 04 — Present Tense I

**Goal:** Say what you do. Ten verbs, one formula, and the sentence patterns that carry you through the next month.

---

3. What ending does ه take before ezâfe?

## 1. Grammar (16 min)

### 1a. The formula

**می‌ + present stem + personal ending**

The endings are the same ones from Lesson 01: **‑م، ‑ی، ‑د، ‑یم، ‑ید، ‑ند**. You already know them.

The only new thing is the **stem** — and the stems are unpredictable. This is the central fact of Persian verbs.

### 1b. Stems are vocabulary, not rules

Every Persian verb has two stems, a present and a past. You cannot reliably derive the present stem from the infinitive. **Learn the stem alongside the infinitive, as one vocabulary item.**

| Infinitive | Meaning | Present stem | 1sg |
|---|---|---|---|
| رفتن (raftan) | to go | **رو** rav‑ | می‌روم |
| آمدن (âmadan) | to come | **آ** â‑ | می‌آیم |
| خوردن (khordan) | to eat, to drink | **خور** khor‑ | می‌خورم |
| دیدن (didan) | to see | **بین** bin‑ | می‌بینم |
| گفتن (goftan) | to say | **گو** gu‑ | می‌گویم |
| خواندن (khândan) | to read, to sing | **خوان** khân‑ | می‌خوانم |
| نوشتن (neveshtan) | to write | **نویس** nevis‑ | می‌نویسم |
| کردن (kardan) | to do, to make | **کن** kon‑ | می‌کنم |
| خواستن (khâstan) | to want | **خواه** khâh‑ | می‌خواهم |
| دانستن (dânestan) | to know | **دان** dân‑ | می‌دانم |

Look at دیدن → **بین**. Nothing in the infinitive predicts that. Same for گفتن → **گو**. This is normal; there are about 200 simple verbs in Persian and their stems are simply memorized. The good news: once you have the stem, the conjugation is perfectly regular.

### 1c. Full conjugation — رفتن

| | Farsi | Translit |
|---|---|---|
| I go | می‌روم | miravam |
| you go *(sg)* | می‌روی | miravi |
| he/she goes | می‌رود | miravad |
| we go | می‌رویم | miravim |
| you go *(pl)* | می‌روید | miravid |
| they go | می‌روند | miravand |

**Three notes:**

1. **می‌ is written with a half-space** (نیم‌فاصله), attached but visually separated: می‌روم. Not می روم.
2. **The stress goes on می‌.** **MI**-ravam, not mi-ra-**VAM**. This is unusual — nouns stress the last syllable, but verb prefixes steal the stress. Getting this right is a large part of sounding Persian.
3. آمدن inserts a ی: می‌آیم، می‌آیی، می‌آید…

### 1d. One tense, two English meanings

Persian present covers both English present tenses:

> من کتاب می‌خوانم = *I read books* **and** *I am reading a book.*

Context decides. There is no separate progressive form you need right now.

### 1e. The داشتن exception

**داشتن** (to have) takes **no می‌** in the present:

| | Farsi | |
|---|---|---|
| I have | دارم | dâram |
| you have | داری | dâri |
| he/she has | دارد | dârad |
| we have | داریم | dârim |
| you have | دارید | dârid |
| they have | دارند | dârand |

Never می‌دارم. A small set of stative verbs behaves this way; داشتن is the one that matters. Full treatment in Lesson 08.

### 1f. Word order

Subject — time — place — object — **verb**.

> من **هر روز** **به دانشگاه** می‌روم.
> man har ruz be dâneshgâh miravam — *I go to the university every day.*
>
> نگار **الان** **چای** می‌خورد.
> Negâr alân chây mikhorad — *Negar is drinking tea right now.*

The verb is last. Everything else is flexible. Subjects are often dropped entirely, since the ending already tells you the person: **می‌روم** alone means "I go."

### Time & direction words

| امروز | emruz | today |
| هر روز | har ruz | every day |
| الان | alân | now |
| همیشه | hamishe | always |
| معمولاً | ma'mulan | usually |
| خیلی | kheyli | very, a lot |
| به | be | to |
| از | az | from |

### Places to go

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| دانشگاه | dâneshgâh | university |
| سر کار | sar-e kâr | to/at work |

### A note on خوردن

Persian uses **خوردن** ("to eat") for drinking too. چای می‌خورم = *I drink tea*. آب می‌خورم = *I drink water*. There's a verb نوشیدن ("to drink") but it sounds bookish. Use خوردن.

### Preview: کردن makes verbs

Persian builds most of its verbs by pairing a noun with **کردن**:

> کار کردن — kâr kardan — *to work* (lit. "to do work") → **کار می‌کنم** — I work
> صحبت کردن — sohbat kardan — *to speak* → **صحبت می‌کنم** — I speak

Note the noun stays put and only کردن conjugates. These are **compound verbs**, the dominant verb type in Persian. Full lesson at L11 — for now, learn these two as fixed items.

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

The colloquial present tense is where written and spoken Persian diverge the most. Two sound rules do almost all the work:

**Rule 1 — ân → un** (you already know this)
**Rule 2 — the 3sg ending ‑د drops and becomes ‑ه**

| Formal | Colloquial | |
|---|---|---|
| می‌روم | می‌رم (miram) | rav‑ contracts to r‑ |
| می‌رود | می‌ره (mire) | |
| می‌روند | می‌رن (miran) | |
| می‌آیم | میام (miyâm) | |
| می‌گویم | می‌گم (migam) | gu‑ → g‑ |
| می‌گوید | می‌گه (mige) | |
| می‌خوانم | می‌خونم (mikhunam) | ân → un |
| می‌خواهم | می‌خوام (mikhâm) | |
| می‌دانم | می‌دونم (midunam) | |
| می‌کند | می‌کنه (mikone) | |
| دارد | داره (dâre) | |
| می‌خورد | می‌خوره (mikhore) | |

So *"She says she wants to go"* is written می‌گوید که می‌خواهد برود and spoken **می‌گه که می‌خواد بره**.

**Bonus — the spoken progressive.** For "I'm doing it *right now*," colloquial Persian adds داشتن in front:

> **دارم می‌رم** — dâram miram — *I'm going (right now)*
> **داره می‌خوره** — dâre mikhore — *he's eating (right now)*

You'll hear this constantly. It has no formal written equivalent.

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 6 minutes.**

> **Part 1 — Stress check.** Matt's biggest pronunciation risk this week is stressing the wrong syllable in verbs. The stress belongs on **می**. Have him say these and correct any that come out flat or end-stressed:
> می‌روم، می‌خورم، می‌بینم، می‌نویسم، می‌خواهم
>
> **Part 2 — Formal → colloquial.** Say each of these to him in your natural speech and have him write down what he hears, then tell him the written form:
> ۱. من هر روز می‌رم سر کار.
> ۲. نگار داره چای می‌خوره.
> ۳. اونا کتاب می‌خونن.
> ۴. من نمی‌دونم.
>
> **Part 3 — Rapid Q&A.** Ask him these and let him answer in full sentences:
> هر روز کجا می‌ری؟ · چی می‌خوری؟ · چی می‌خونی؟

## 7. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Conjugation drill (8 min).** Write all six forms, in script, for رفتن، خوردن، دیدن، گفتن، داشتن. Then write the colloquial 1sg and 3sg for each.

**H2 — Verb card set (4 min).** Make a card for each of the ten verbs with **three** faces: infinitive / present stem / 1sg. You must be able to produce the stem from the infinitive cold. This is the highest-leverage flashcard set in the course — every future tense builds on these.

**H3 — Translation (5 min).**

2. Negar goes to work.

**H4 — Paragraph (3 min).** Write five sentences about your day in script. Then rewrite all five in colloquial form.

## Tutor notes

- The stem table is the real content of this lesson. If the student can conjugate رفتن but can't produce بین from دیدن cold, the lesson didn't land. Test stems in isolation, not inside sentences.
- Verb-final word order will slip under cognitive load — the moment the sentence gets longer than four words, English order reappears. Expect it; correct it without commentary.
- Do not introduce the subjunctive even if the student produces "I want to go." Give them می‌خواهم بروم as an unanalyzed chunk if needed and defer to L21–22.
- H3 item 5 deliberately asks for negation a lesson early. Let them guess; it makes L05 land better.
- Watch for می‌داشتن. It's the most common error of the week.
