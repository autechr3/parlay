## 1. Grammar (14 min)

### 1a. Past stems are free

Unlike present stems, past stems are completely predictable:

> **Drop the ن from the infinitive.**

| Infinitive | Past stem |
|---|---|
| رفتن | **رفت** |
| خوردن | **خورد** |
| دیدن | **دید** |
| گفتن | **گفت** |
| خواندن | **خواند** |
| نوشتن | **نوشت** |
| کردن | **کرد** |
| داشتن | **داشت** |
| بودن | **بود** |
| خواستن | **خواست** |
| دانستن | **دانست** |
| خریدن | **خرید** |
| دادن | **داد** |
| گرفتن | **گرفت** |
| آمدن | **آمد** |
| شدن | **شد** |

No exceptions. After the pain of present stems, this is a gift.

### 1b. The endings — with one twist

| Person | Ending | رفتن |
|---|---|---|
| I | ‑م | رفتم (raftam) |
| you *(sg)* | ‑ی | رفتی (rafti) |
| he/she | **— nothing** | **رفت** (raft) |
| we | ‑یم | رفتیم (raftim) |
| you *(pl)* | ‑ید | رفتید (raftid) |
| they | ‑ند | رفتند (raftand) |

**The third-person singular takes no ending at all.** The bare past stem *is* the verb.

> او رفت. — *He went.*
> نگار کتاب را خواند. — *Negar read the book.*

Contrast with the present, which does have an ending there (می‌رو**د**). This asymmetry is the one thing to drill.

### 1c. No می‌ in the simple past

> ✅ رفتم — *I went*
> ❌ می‌رفتم — this is a *different* tense ("I used to go" / "I was going") — Lesson 13.

### 1d. Negation

**نـ** on the front, stressed:

> **ن**رفتم — **na**raftam — *I didn't go*
> **ن**دیدم — nadidam — *I didn't see*
> **ن**بود — nabud — *it wasn't*

### 1e. بودن in the past

| بودم | budam | I was |
| بودی | budi | you were |
| بود | bud | he/she/it was |
| بودیم | budim | we were |
| بودید | budid | you were |
| بودند | budand | they were |

> دیروز خانه بودم. — *I was at home yesterday.*
> ماشینش قدیمی بود. — *His car was old.*

### 1f. Word order is unchanged

Everything you learned still applies — را, prepositions, verb-final:

> دیروز کتابم را به نگار دادم.
> *Yesterday I gave my book to Negar.*

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

Good news: **the past tense is the closest the two registers get.** رفتم is رفتم in both. Only the usual sound rules apply:

| Formal | Colloquial |
|---|---|
| آمدم | اومدم (umadam) |
| خواندم | خوندم (khundam) |
| دانستم | دونستم (dunestam) |
| توانستم | تونستم (tunestam) |
| رفتند | رفتن (raftan) — 3pl drops the ‑د |
| رفتید | رفتین (raftin) |
| گفتند | گفتن |

**The 3rd plural ‑ند → ‑ن is the one to internalize.** رفتن (raftan) in speech means *they went* — which is also, confusingly, exactly how the infinitive رفتن is spelled. Context sorts it out, but it's worth knowing why you'll hear it.

**High-frequency past phrases:**
- **چی گفتی؟** — *What did you say?*
- **کجا رفتی؟** — *Where did you go?*
- **نفهمیدم** — *I didn't understand* — you will need this one a lot
- **یادم رفت** — *I forgot* (lit. "it went from my memory")

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 10 minutes. This is the Unit 1 milestone — worth the extra time.**

> Matt has finished the first two weeks. Please do a real conversation, not a drill.
>
> **Part 1 (5 min) — Ask him about yesterday** entirely in Farsi, speaking naturally. Suggested questions:
> دیروز چیکار کردی؟ · کجا رفتی؟ · صبح ساعت چند بیدار شدی؟ · چی خوردی؟ · با کی حرف زدی؟ · دیشب چی خوندی؟
>
> Let him struggle. Don't switch to English. If he's stuck, rephrase more simply rather than translating.
>
> **Part 2 (3 min) — Now you tell him** about your yesterday, in simple Farsi, for about a minute. He should be able to follow roughly half. Ask him what he understood.
>
> **Part 3 (2 min) — Honest feedback.** Rate him 1–5 on: pronunciation, whether the ezâfe is audible, whether you could follow him without effort, and whether he sounded like a textbook or a person. Tell him the single thing that would most improve how he sounds.

## Tutor notes

- **The zero-ending 3sg is the whole grammatical point.** Students write رفتد for weeks. Drill B and D1 target it directly; repeat them until reflexive.
- Don't let the assessment turn into a re-teach. Score it, note the gaps, move on. The gap list (H4) is the deliverable, not a perfect score.
- If Part A scores below 8/10, insert a consolidation session before L11 rather than pushing forward. Unit 2 assumes all of this is automatic.
- The Negar conversation in Part 1 is the first unscripted exchange of the course. Prepare the student for it to feel bad — that's expected and not a signal of failure.
- After this lesson, generate Unit 2 (L11–L20) using `GENERATE_LESSONS.md`, and feed it the student's Part E ratings so weak areas get folded into the warm-ups.
