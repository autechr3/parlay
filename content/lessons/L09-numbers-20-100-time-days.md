# Lesson 09 — Numbers 20–100, Time & Days

**Goal:** Say any number up to 100, tell time, name the days, and make a plan.

---

## 0. Warm-up (3 min)

1. Possessive suffixes, all six, on خانه.
2. Translate: *Our house is near the university. There's no chair here.*
3. Which of these take ezâfe: به، روی، از، کنار، با?

---

## 1. Numbers 20–100 (8 min)

### The tens

| # | Farsi | Translit |
|---|---|---|
| ۲۰ | بیست | bist |
| ۳۰ | سی | si |
| ۴۰ | چهل | chehel |
| ۵۰ | پنجاه | panjâh |
| ۶۰ | شصت | shast |
| ۷۰ | هفتاد | haftâd |
| ۸۰ | هشتاد | hashtâd |
| ۹۰ | نود | navad |
| ۱۰۰ | صد | sad |

Most are transparently built from the units: هفت→هفتاد، هشت→هشتاد، پنج→پنجاه. **سی (30), چهل (40), شصت (60), نود (90)** are the four you'll have to memorize outright.

### Compounds — join with و

**و** here is pronounced **‑o‑**, not *va*:

> ۲۱ — بیست **و** یک — bist-**o**-yek
> ۴۵ — چهل و پنج — chehel-o-panj
> ۹۹ — نود و نه — navad-o-noh
> ۷۶ — هفتاد و شش — haftâd-o-shesh

Every number from 21 to 99 works this way, no exceptions. It's the easiest number system you'll ever learn.

### And your age

> چند سالت است؟ → **چهل و دو سالم است.** — *I'm forty-two.*
> Colloquially: چند سالته؟ → **چهل و دو سالمه.**

## 2. Days of the week (6 min)

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| شنبه | shanbe | Saturday |
| یکشنبه | yekshanbe | Sunday |
| دوشنبه | doshanbe | Monday |
| سه‌شنبه | seshanbe | Tuesday |
| چهارشنبه | chahârshanbe | Wednesday |
| پنجشنبه | panjshanbe | Thursday |
| جمعه | jom'e | Friday |

**The system is beautifully simple:** شنبه is the anchor, and the rest are literally "one-shanbe, two-shanbe, three-shanbe…" You already know the numbers, so you already know the days.

**Culturally important:** the Iranian week **starts on شنبه (Saturday)**. **جمعه (Friday) is the day off** — the equivalent of Sunday. پنجشنبه is often a half day, so the Iranian weekend is Thursday afternoon + Friday.

So when Negar says "we'll do it شنبه," she means the start of the work week, not the weekend.

## 3. Telling time (10 min)

### The question

> **ساعت چند است؟** — sâ'at chand ast? — *What time is it?*
> Colloquially: **ساعت چنده؟** — sâ'at chande?

### The answers

| Persian | English |
|---|---|
| ساعت سه است. | It's three o'clock. |
| ساعت سه **و نیم** است. | It's three thirty. *(نیم = half)* |
| ساعت سه **و ربع** است. | It's three fifteen. *(ربع = quarter)* |
| ساعت سه **و بیست دقیقه** است. | It's three twenty. |
| **یک ربع به** چهار | quarter to four |
| **بیست دقیقه به** چهار | twenty to four |

**Pattern:** minutes past use **و** ("and"), minutes to use **به** ("to"). Same logic as English.

### Parts of the day

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| صبح | sobh | morning |
| ظهر | zohr | noon |
| بعدازظهر | ba'd-az-zohr | afternoon |
| عصر | asr | late afternoon / early evening |
| شب | shab | night |
| نصف شب | nesf-e shab | midnight |

> ساعت هشتِ صبح — *8 a.m.* · ساعت هشتِ شب — *8 p.m.*

Iran uses the 24-hour clock in writing and schedules, but people speak in 12-hour time with these day-part words attached.

### Time vocabulary

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| ساعت | sâ'at | hour, clock, watch |
| دقیقه | daqiqe | minute |
| هفته | hafte | week |
| ماه | mâh | month, moon |
| سال | sâl | year |
| امروز | emruz | today |
| دیروز | diruz | yesterday |
| فردا | fardâ | tomorrow |
| امشب | emshab | tonight |

### Saying when something happens

No preposition needed before a clock time:

> **ساعت هشت** می‌آیم. — *I'm coming at eight.*
> **شنبه** به دانشگاه می‌روم. — *I go to the university on Saturday.*
> **فردا صبح** کار می‌کنم. — *I work tomorrow morning.*

## 4. Guided practice (8 min)

**Drill A — Read aloud.** ۲۳ · ۴۷ · ۶۱ · ۸۸ · ۳۵ · ۹۹ · ۷۲ · ۱۰۰ · ۵۴

**Drill B — Write in Persian digits and in words.** 26 · 39 · 58 · 64 · 91

**Drill C — What time?** Say each in Farsi: 3:00 · 4:30 · 7:15 · 9:20 · 5:45 · 11:00 p.m. · 8:00 a.m.

**Drill D — Translate.**
1. What time is it?
2. It's half past six.
3. I go to work at eight in the morning.
4. On Monday I'm going to Negar's house.
5. I'm forty-two years old.
6. There are seven days in a week. *(hint: هست)*
7. Tomorrow is Friday.

**Drill E — Days.** Say all seven in order, then backwards. Then: what day is two days after دوشنبه?

**Drill F — Fix the error.**
1. بیست یک
2. ساعت سه نیم است.
3. در ساعت هشت می‌آیم.
4. شنبه weekend است.

## 5. Production (5 min)

**Your week.** Write seven sentences — one per day — saying what you do and at what time. Use a different verb each day.

> Model: شنبه ساعت هشتِ صبح سر کار می‌روم.

## 6. How Negar Actually Says It

| Formal | Colloquial |
|---|---|
| ساعت چند است؟ | ساعت چنده؟ |
| ساعت سه است | ساعت سه‌ست / سه‌ئه |
| چهل و دو سالم است | چهل و دو سالمه |
| نصف شب | نصفه‌شب |
| بعدازظهر | بعدازظهر / عصر |
| فردا صبح | فردا صبح |
| پنجشنبه | پنج‌شنبه |

**Number habits in speech:**
- Iranians often say **صد** for "a hundred" loosely, meaning "loads" — صد بار گفتم = "I've said it a hundred times."
- In compounds the **و** is genuinely just a fast *o*: چهل و پنج comes out as **chehelopanj**, one word. Don't over-pronounce it.

**The Persian calendar.** Everything above is the Gregorian week, which Iran uses for the days. But the *dates* run on the **Solar Hijri** calendar — the year 2026 is mostly **۱۴۰۵**, and the year begins at **نوروز** (Nowruz) on the spring equinox, around March 21. Negar's family will think in both. Full lesson at L28; for now, know that if someone says "ماه فروردین" they mean roughly late March–April.

## 7. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 6 minutes.**

> **Part 1 — Number dictation.** Say fifteen numbers between 20 and 100 at natural speed. Matt writes them in Persian digits. Then reverse it: he says numbers, you write them — this catches his pronunciation errors.
>
> **Part 2 — What time?** Ask **ساعت چنده؟** at six different fake times and have him answer. Then ask him:
> صبح ساعت چند بیدار می‌شی؟ · ساعت چند می‌ری سر کار؟ · شنبه چیکار می‌کنی؟
>
> **Part 3 — Culture check.** Tell him what your family's actual weekly rhythm was in Iran — which day was the day off, when the work week started, what happens on جمعه. Two minutes of this is worth more than the table in the lesson.

## 8. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Number writing (5 min).** Write these in Persian digits and in words: 24, 37, 42, 56, 68, 73, 85, 96.

**H2 — Clock drill (5 min).** Draw eight clock faces at random times. Write each time in Farsi script.

**H3 — Weekly schedule (6 min).** Write your actual schedule for the coming week in Farsi — day, time, activity. Minimum seven lines. Keep it; you'll reuse it in L13 for the past tense.

**H4 — Ask Negar (4 min).** Ask her, in Farsi: what time she wakes up, what time she goes to work, what she does on Friday. Write her answers in script.

## Tutor notes

- Number dictation from a native speaker at natural speed is the real skill; reading numbers off a page is not. Prioritize the Negar drill.
- Watch for over-pronouncing و in compounds — it should be a fast unstressed *o*, effectively swallowed.
- The Saturday-start week catches everyone. Test it directly: "if Negar says شنبه, is that the weekend?"
- Don't open the Solar Hijri calendar beyond the note here. It's a full lesson (L28) and will eat the hour.
- H3's schedule gets reused in L13 for past-tense conversion — make sure the student actually keeps it.
