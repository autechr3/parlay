# Lesson 03 — Ezâfe, the Persian Glue

**Goal:** Build noun phrases. This is the most structurally important lesson in the first month. Every Persian sentence you ever write will contain this.

---

## 0. Warm-up (4 min)

1. Pluralize in script: شهر، بچه، مرد، پنجره.
2. Translate: *These are books. That is a car. Six chairs.*
3. Say the colloquial form of این چیست؟

---

## 1. Grammar (16 min)

### 1a. What ezâfe is

Persian links a noun to whatever describes it with a short **‑e** sound. That's it. That's the whole mechanism.

> **کتابِ خوب** — ketâb**-e** khub — *the good book*
> **کتابِ من** — ketâb**-e** man — *my book*
> **ماشینِ نگار** — mâshin**-e** Negâr — *Negar's car*

Three things to absorb immediately:

1. **It isn't written.** That little ِ mark above is a teaching aid. Real Persian writes کتاب خوب and expects you to know. This is why reading Persian aloud requires understanding the sentence — you have to know the structure to know how to pronounce it.
2. **The describing word comes second.** Persian is *book good*, *car Negar*. Backwards from English, consistently.
3. **Possession runs backwards too.** English says *Negar's car*; Persian says *car-of Negar*. The thing owned comes first, always.

### 1b. What ezâfe connects

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| noun + adjective | کتابِ بزرگ | the big book |
| noun + possessor noun | ماشینِ نگار | Negar's car |
| noun + possessor pronoun | خانهٔ من | my house |
| title + name | آقای احمدی | Mr. Ahmadi |
| first + last name | نگارِ رضایی | Negar Rezaei |
| location word + noun | رویِ میز | on the table |

That last row is why Persian prepositions look strange — روی isn't really "on," it's "surface-of." You'll meet the full set in Lesson 08.

### 1c. After a vowel, ezâfe becomes ‑ye

If the first word ends in a vowel sound, the linking vowel gets a **ی** to lean on.

| Word ends in… | Written | Pronounced |
|---|---|---|
| ه (silent) — خانه | خانه‌ی من *or* خانهٔ من | khâne**-ye** man |
| ا — آقا | آقای احمدی | âqâ**-ye** Ahmadi |
| و — دانشجو | دانشجوی خوب | dâneshju**-ye** khub |
| ی — صندلی | صندلی من *(unchanged)* | sandali**-ye** man |

Both خانه‌ی and خانهٔ are correct. **خانه‌ی is the more common modern typing convention** — use that one and be consistent.

### 1d. Chains

Ezâfe stacks. Each link is another ‑e.

> کتابِ خوبِ من — ketâb-e khub-e man — *my good book*
> ماشینِ قرمزِ دوستِ من — mâshin-e qermez-e dust-e man — *my friend's red car*
> خانه‌ی بزرگِ پدرِ نگار — *Negar's father's big house*

Order inside the chain: **noun → adjective → possessor.** Multiple adjectives simply queue up: مردِ پیرِ خوب.

### 1e. The contrast that matters most

Look carefully:

> **کتابِ خوب** — ketâb**-e** khub — *the good book* (a phrase — not a sentence)
> **کتاب خوب است.** — ketâb khub ast — *The book is good.* (a full sentence)

**Identical letters. The ezâfe is the only difference.** Add the ‑e and you get a noun phrase; leave it out and add a verb and you get a statement.

This is the single most common comprehension failure for learners reading Persian aloud, and the reason ezâfe gets its own hour today. Drill B below is built entirely around it.

*Preview — the one exception:* superlatives go **before** the noun and take no ezâfe: بهترین کتاب (*the best book*). Full treatment in Lesson 17.

## 2. Vocabulary — adjectives (7 min)

| Farsi | Translit | English |

| بزرگ | bozorg | big |
| کوچک | kuchak | small |
| جدید | jadid | new |
| قدیمی | qadimi | old *(things)* |
| پیر | pir | old *(people)* |
| جوان | javân | young |
| زیبا | zibâ | beautiful |

## 3. Guided practice (10 min)

**Drill A — Build the phrase (script).**
1. a big house
2. Negar's car
3. my book
4. an interesting book
5. the young teacher
6. my friend's small house
7. Mr. Ahmadi's new car
8. Negar's beautiful house

**Drill B — Phrase or sentence?** Read each aloud, say whether it's a phrase or a sentence, and translate.
۱. ماشینِ گران
۲. ماشین گران است.
۳. خانه‌ی بزرگ
۴. خانه بزرگ است.
۵. کتابِ من
۶. کتاب مالِ من است.

**Drill C — ‑e or ‑ye?** Say how each links: کتاب + من / خانه + ما / آقا + رضایی / دانشجو + جوان / شهر + بزرگ / صندلی + تو.

**Drill D — Correct the error.**
1. بزرگ خانه
2. نگار ماشین
3. خانه من *(pronunciation — what's missing in speech?)*
4. من کتاب خوب است.

**Drill E — Read aloud, no help.** These have no vowel marks, exactly as real Persian is written. Decide where the ezâfe goes:
> دوستِ خوبِ من ایرانی است. شهرِ بزرگِ ما زیبا است. کتابِ جدیدِ معلم گران است.

---

## 4. Production (5 min)

Describe three things you own, each with at least one adjective, using an ezâfe chain. Then describe one thing belonging to Negar. Write in script, read aloud.

> Model: ماشینِ قدیمیِ من کوچک است. — *My old car is small.*

Notice that model sentence has **both**: an ezâfe chain (ماشینِ قدیمیِ من) *and* a predicate (کوچک است). That's the target shape.

---

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

Ezâfe survives fully intact in speech — it's not a formal-register thing. But three habits differ:

| Formal | Colloquial | |
|---|---|---|
| کتابِ من | کتابم | possessive **suffixes** replace ezâfe+pronoun constantly (Lesson 08) |
| خانه‌ی ما | خونمون | khune-mun |
| زیبا | قشنگ / خوشگل | زیبا is a bit literary; قشنگ and خوشگل are what people say |
| آسان | راحت / آسون | راحت is far more common |
| کوچک | کوچیک | kuchak → kuchik |
| قرمز | قرمز | *(fine as-is)* |

**The pattern to internalize:** in speech, *my book* is more often **کتابم** than **کتابِ من**. Both are correct; کتابِ من adds emphasis — *my* book, as opposed to yours. You'll learn the suffix set properly in Lesson 08; for now just stop being surprised when you hear it.

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 5 minutes. This is the most useful drill of the first two weeks.**

> Persian doesn't write the ezâfe vowel, so Matt has to learn to *hear* it. Please read these six items aloud at normal speed, in a shuffled order, and have him tell you after each one whether he heard the linking **‑e** or not:
>
> ۱. کتابِ خوب  ۲. کتاب خوبه  ۳. ماشینِ بزرگ  ۴. ماشین بزرگه
> ۵. خانه‌ی قشنگ  ۶. خونه قشنگه
>
> Then read these three sentences at **natural speed** and ask him to translate:
> ۱. ماشینِ قدیمیِ دوستِ من خیلی گرونه.
> ۲. خونه‌ی بزرگِ شما خیلی قشنگه.
> ۳. کتابِ جدیدِ معلم خیلی سخته.
>
> (خیلی = kheyli = "very" — a freebie, he hasn't learned it yet.)

## Tutor notes

- **This lesson gates the whole course.** If the student can't reliably produce ezâfe chains, do not proceed to L04 — re-run drills A and B with fresh vocabulary instead. Losing a day here saves a month later.
- The failure mode is silent: students *write* ezâfe correctly (since it's invisible, they can't get it wrong) while never *pronouncing* it. Always make them read aloud. Always.
- Second failure mode: English possession order — نگار ماشین for "Negar's car." Correct instantly, every time.
- H2's minimal-pair table is the highest-value exercise in Unit 1. If homework gets cut for time, cut H1 and keep H2.
- If the student asks about بهترین/superlatives, confirm it's the one construction that precedes the noun and defer to L17.
