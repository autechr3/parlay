# Lesson 02 — Nouns, Plurals & Pointing

**Goal:** Name objects around you, make them plural, and say "this is a X / these are X."

---

## 0. Warm-up (4 min)

Rapid-fire, script only, no notes:
1. Conjugate بودن in all six persons with خوب.
2. Translate: *I am an engineer. She is Iranian. We are teachers.*
3. What's the colloquial form of او آمریکایی است؟

---

## 1. Grammar (14 min)

### 1a. Plural with ‑ها

Add **‑ها** to any noun. Animate, inanimate, abstract — it always works.

| Singular | Plural | |
|---|---|---|
| کتاب | کتاب‌ها | ketâb-hâ — books |
| ماشین | ماشین‌ها | mâshin-hâ — cars |
| زن | زن‌ها | zan-hâ — women |
| خانه | خانه‌ها | khâne-hâ — houses |

The little gap (ZWNJ, نیم‌فاصله) between the noun and ها is standard modern typing. کتابها written solid is also acceptable and common. Either is fine; be consistent.

> **‑ها is never wrong.** If you remember nothing else about Persian plurals, remember that. Everything below is refinement.

### 1b. Plural with ‑ان

**‑ان** (‑ân) pluralizes **animate beings** and belongs to a more formal or written register.

| Singular | Plural ‑ان | Plural ‑ها |
|---|---|---|
| مرد (man) | مردان | مردها |
| زن (woman) | زنان | زن‌ها |
| دانشجو (student) | دانشجو**ی**ان | دانشجوها |
| درخت (tree) | درختان | درخت‌ها |

Both columns are correct. **‑ان feels literary; ‑ها feels normal.** In speech you will hear ‑ها almost exclusively. Recognize ‑ان when reading; produce ‑ها when speaking.

Note دانشجو → دانشجو**ی**ان: a **ی** is inserted after a vowel to break it up. Same reflex you'll see everywhere in Persian.

*You will also meet Arabic "broken" plurals in formal text — معلم → معلمین، کتاب → کتب. Recognize them later; never form them yourself. Persian speakers say معلم‌ها.*

### 1c. The rule that trips everyone: numbers kill the plural

After a number, the noun stays **singular**.

> ✅ سه کتاب — se ketâb — three books
> ❌ سه کتاب‌ها

This is the opposite of English and it never varies. Same for چند (chand, "several/how many"): چند کتاب.

### 1d. Indefinite — "a / some"

Persian has no article, but two optional ways to mark indefiniteness:

1. **یک** before the noun — یک کتاب (yek ketâb) — "a book" / "one book"
2. **‑ی** attached after the noun — کتابی (ketâbi) — "a book" / "some book"

Both are common; ‑ی has a vaguer, "a certain book" flavor. You can even stack them: یک کتابی.

If you say nothing at all — just کتاب — that's also fine and often best. Persian is comfortable leaving it unmarked.

### 1e. Pointing: این and آن

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| این | in | this / these |
| آن | ân | that / those |

They **never change form**, singular or plural:

> این کتاب — this book
> این کتاب‌ها — these books
> آن ماشین — that car
> آن ماشین‌ها — those cars

**Standing alone** (as pronouns, not before a noun), they take the plural:

> این کتاب است. — This is a book.
> این‌ها کتاب هستند. — These are books.
> آن چیست؟ — What is that?

**Key sentence pattern for this lesson:**

> این چیست؟ — in chist? — What is this?
> این یک کتاب است. — in yek ketâb ast. — This is a book.
> آن‌ها میز هستند. — ânhâ miz hastand. — Those are tables.

(چیست = چه + است, "what is." Fixed form, learn it whole.)

## 3. Guided practice (10 min)

**Drill A — Pluralize (script).** Give the ‑ها plural of: کتاب، خانه، زن، شهر، بچه، دوست، پنجره.

**Drill B — ‑ان or ‑ها?** Which of these can take ‑ان, and why? مرد، میز، درخت، صندلی، زن، تلفن.

**Drill C — Translate into Farsi script.**
1. This is a book.
2. That is a car.
3. These are chairs.
4. Those are children.
5. Three books.
6. This house is good.

**Drill D — Fix the error.**
1. پنج کتاب‌ها
2. این‌ها کتاب است.
3. این ماشین‌ها خوب هستم.

**Drill E — Point and name.** Look around the room you're in. Name six objects you can see, in script, with این or آن as appropriate. Use any word you know; if you don't know it, that's a vocabulary gap worth logging.

## 4. Production (5 min)

Describe your desk in five sentences using only این/آن + noun + بودن. Example:

> این یک میز است. آن صندلی است. این کتاب‌ها خوب هستند.

Read it aloud. Then rewrite all five in the colloquial forms from §5.

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

| Formal | Colloquial | |
|---|---|---|
| آن | اون | ân → un — the rule from L01, again |
| آن‌ها | اونا | |
| این‌ها | اینا | |
| کتاب‌ها | کتابا | **the plural ‑ها becomes just ‑ا in speech** |
| خانه / خانه‌ها | خونه / خونه‌ها | ân→un applies *inside* words too |
| نان | نون | |
| یک کتاب | یه کتاب | yek → **ye** before a noun |
| این چیست؟ | این چیه؟ | in chi-ye? |
| آن چیست؟ | اون چیه؟ | |

So the sentence *این‌ها کتاب‌های خوبی هستند* comes out of her mouth as **اینا کتابای خوبی‌ان**. That's not slang — that's just how Persian is spoken by everyone.

**Two more you'll hear constantly:**
- **چیه؟** (chi-ye?) — "what is it?" — the single most useful two-syllable question in the language.
- **اینو ببین** — "look at this" — you'll meet the ‑و in Lesson 07.

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 5 minutes.**

> Matt is learning plurals. Please do this:
>
> **Part 1 —** Point at six things in the room and ask him **این چیه؟** Let him answer in Farsi. He only knows about 25 nouns, so pick easy ones (کتاب، میز، صندلی، در، پنجره، تلفن، آب، چای).
>
> **Part 2 —** Say each of these to him the way you'd naturally say it, and have him write down what he hears in script:
> ۱. اینا کتابن.
> ۲. اون ماشین منه.
> ۳. سه تا صندلی
> ۴. خونه‌ی ما
>
> Tell him which written plurals he'd never actually hear you say out loud.

## 7. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Copying drill (6 min).** Write each of the 18 new nouns twice: once singular, once with ‑ها. Script only.

**H2 — Translation (6 min).**
1. This is a house.
2. Those are windows.
3. Four chairs.
4. This water is good.
5. These men are Iranian.
6. That child is tired.

**H3 — Object hunt (5 min).** Walk through your home. Write ten sentences of the form `این/آن + noun + است` for things you can actually see. Look up any two words you're missing and add them to your deck.

**H4 — Colloquial rewrite (3 min).** Convert to spoken form: آن‌ها کتاب‌ها هستند / این خانه است / یک ماشین.

**A:** کتاب‌ها، خانه‌ها، زن‌ها، شهرها، بچه‌ها، دوست‌ها، پنجره‌ها

**B:** ‑ان works for مرد (مردان)، درخت (درختان)، زن (زنان) — animate/living. میز، صندلی، تلفن are inanimate objects → ‑ها only.

**D:**
1. **پنج کتاب** — number kills the plural.
2. **این‌ها کتاب هستند** — plural subject needs plural verb.
3. **این ماشین‌ها خوب هستند** — verb must agree with the subject, not with the speaker.

## Tutor notes

- Drill C item 6 (این خانه خوب است) quietly sets up Lesson 03's core contrast: این خانه**ی** خوب ("this good house") vs این خانه خوب است ("this house is good"). Don't explain it yet — just note whether the student reads it with or without the ezâfe vowel. Their instinct here predicts how hard L03 will be.
- If the student writes سه کتاب‌ها even once, drill five more number+noun pairs immediately. This error calcifies fast.
- Push back on any transliteration-only answers.
- Nouns are cheap and this lesson is light on grammar — if there's time left, front-load vocabulary rather than adding grammar. L03 is heavy and they'll want the headroom.
