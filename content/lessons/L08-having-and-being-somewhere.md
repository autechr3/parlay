# Lesson 08 — Having & Being Somewhere

**Goal:** Say what's yours, and say where things are. Two systems, both high-frequency.

---

## 0. Warm-up (3 min)

1. را or no را: *I see the car / I buy bread / I know Negar / I read books.*
2. Translate: من ماشینِ جدیدِ نگار را می‌بینم.
3. Colloquial form of کتاب را می‌خوانم؟

---

## 1. Grammar A — Possessive suffixes (12 min)

In Lesson 03 you learned to say *my book* as **کتابِ من**. There's a shorter way, and it's what people actually use.

### 1a. The suffix set

| Suffix | Translit | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ‑م | ‑am | my | کتابم |
| ‑ت | ‑at | your *(sg)* | کتابت |
| ‑ش | ‑ash | his / her / its | کتابش |
| ‑مان | ‑emân | our | کتابمان |
| ‑تان | ‑etân | your *(pl/formal)* | کتابتان |
| ‑شان | ‑eshân | their | کتابشان |

Look at those endings again: **‑م، ‑ت، ‑ش، ‑مان، ‑تان، ‑شان**. Not identical to the verb endings, but the same family. Persian reuses a small set of building blocks everywhere.

### 1b. After a vowel, add ا

Words ending in ه or a vowel need a buffer:

> خانه + م → **خانه‌ام** (khâne-am) — *my house*
> خانه + ت → **خانه‌ات** · خانه + ش → **خانه‌اش**
> دانشجو + م → **دانشجویم**

### 1c. Suffix vs ezâfe — when to use which

Both are correct. They differ in emphasis:

> **کتابم** — *my book* — neutral, default, what you say 90% of the time.
> **کتابِ من** — ***my*** *book* — contrastive. As opposed to yours.

Use the suffix unless you're making a point.

### 1d. Suffixes stack with adjectives and را

The suffix goes on the **last** element of the noun phrase, and را comes after everything:

> ماشینِ قدیمی**م** را فروختم. — *I sold my old car.*
> خانه‌ی بزرگ**شان** را دیدم. — *I saw their big house.*

## 2. Grammar B — داشتن and هست (8 min)

### 2a. داشتن, again

No می‑, positive or negative:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| دارم / ندارم | dâram / nadâram | I have / don't have |
| داری / نداری | | you have |
| دارد / ندارد | | he/she has |
| داریم / نداریم | | we have |
| دارید / ندارید | | you have |
| دارند / ندارند | | they have |

### 2b. هست — "there is"

Persian distinguishes *being somewhere* from *existing somewhere*:

> کتاب روی میز **است**. — *The book is on the table.* (you know which book)
> روی میز یک کتاب **هست**. — *There's a book on the table.* (introducing it)

**هست** asserts existence — English "there is / there are." **است** links a known subject to a location.

The negative of both is **نیست**:

> اینجا صندلی **نیست**. — *There's no chair here.*
> کتابم اینجا **نیست**. — *My book isn't here.*

Plural: هستند / نیستند.

> در اتاق سه تا صندلی **هست**. — *There are three chairs in the room.*
> *(Persian commonly keeps هست singular here — both هست and هستند are heard.)*

---

## 3. Grammar C — Prepositions (8 min)

Persian prepositions come in two flavors, and knowing which is which saves you from a very common mistake.

### 3a. Plain prepositions — no ezâfe

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| در | dar | in *(formal)* |
| تو | tu | in *(everyday)* |
| به | be | to |
| از | az | from |
| با | bâ | with |
| بی | bi | without |
| تا | tâ | until, as far as |
| برای | barâye | for |

> **به** دانشگاه می‌روم. · **از** خانه می‌آیم. · **با** نگار می‌روم.

### 3b. Ezâfe prepositions — these are really nouns

These take the linking ‑e, because they're literally nouns: روی = "surface," زیر = "underside."

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| رویِ | ru-ye | on |
| زیرِ | zir-e | under |
| کنارِ | kenâr-e | next to |
| پشتِ | posht-e | behind |
| جلویِ | jelo-ye | in front of |
| بینِ | beyn-e | between |
| داخلِ | dâkhel-e | inside |
| نزدیکِ | nazdik-e | near |

> کتاب **رویِ** میز است. — *The book is on the table.*
> ماشین **جلویِ** خانه است. — *The car is in front of the house.*
> نگار **کنارِ** من می‌نشیند. — *Negar sits next to me.*

**The mistake to avoid:** dropping the ezâfe on this second group. روی میز without the linking vowel isn't "on the table" — it's not anything. The ezâfe *is* the preposition.

### 3c. Two location words

| اینجا | injâ | here |
| آنجا | ânjâ | there |

## 4. Guided practice (9 min)

**Drill A — Attach the suffix.** All six persons, in script: کتاب، ماشین، خانه، دوست.

**Drill B — Translate.**
1. My book is on the table.
2. Her car is in front of the house.
3. There are four chairs in the room.
4. My friend isn't here.
5. I don't have their address. *(آدرس)*
6. The tea is next to the window.
7. We are going to Negar's house with my friend.
8. There's no water here.

**Drill C — است or هست or نیست?**
1. کتابم روی میز ___.
2. اینجا یک صندلی ___.
3. نگار اینجا ___ ، رفته است.
4. در اتاق سه تا پنجره ___.

**Drill D — Ezâfe or not?** Mark which of these need the linking vowel: به خانه / روی میز / از شهر / زیر صندلی / با نگار / کنار در / برای من / پشت خانه.

**Drill E — Fix the error.**
1. من کتابِ من را می‌خوانم.
2. ماشین روی من است.
3. خانه‌م بزرگ است.
4. اینجا کتاب است. *(intended: "there's a book here")*

---

## 5. Production (5 min)

**Describe your room.** Eight sentences: where five objects are (using the ezâfe prepositions) and who three of them belong to (using possessive suffixes). Then read it aloud and convert to colloquial.

## 6. How Negar Actually Says It

The plural possessive suffixes are the ones that change, and they change *every time*:

| Formal | Colloquial | |
|---|---|---|
| کتابمان | کتابمون | ‑emân → **‑emun** |
| کتابتان | کتابتون | ‑etân → **‑etun** |
| کتابشان | کتابشون | ‑eshân → **‑eshun** |
| خانه‌مان | خونمون | *(the classic — "our house")* |
| خانه‌ام | خونم | |
| در | تو | **تو** replaces در almost entirely in speech |
| به من | بهم (behem) | |
| برای من | برام (barâm) | |
| برای تو | برات (barât) | |
| اینجا / آنجا | اینجا / اونجا | |
| رویِ میز | رو میز | ru-ye often flattens to **ru** |

**Three phrases you'll hear daily:**
- **خونمون** — *our house* — you will hear this constantly from Negar's family.
- **بهم بگو** — *tell me*
- **برات میارم** — *I'll bring it for you*

**Cultural note:** Iranians use plural possessives for family and home far more than English speakers do — خونمون, ماشینمون, خانوادمون. Saying خونم (*my* house) about a family home can sound oddly individualistic. Match what you hear.

## 7. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 6 minutes.**

> **Part 1 — Where is it?** Put an object somewhere and ask Matt **کجاست؟** He answers with a full sentence using a preposition. Do this six times with different positions (on, under, next to, behind, in front of, inside). Correct any missing ezâfe — روی میز must have that linking vowel.
>
> **Part 2 — Whose is it?** Hold up objects and ask **مالِ کیه؟** ("whose is it?"). He answers with a possessive suffix: **کتابمه** / **ماشینشه** / **خونمونه**.
>
> **Part 3 — Say these naturally** and have him write what he hears:
> ۱. کتابت رو میزه.
> ۲. خونمون نزدیکِ دانشگاهه.
> ۳. اینجا صندلی نیست.
> ۴. برات چایی میارم.

## 8. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Suffix drill (6 min).** Six persons × five nouns (کتاب، ماشین، خانه، دوست، اسم), in script. Then all thirty again in colloquial.

**H2 — Room map (6 min).** Draw a rough map of a room in your house. Label eight items in Farsi script. Then write eight sentences saying where each one is relative to another.

**H3 — Translation (5 min).**
1. Our house is near the university.
2. Their car is behind the house.
3. Is your book on the table?
4. There's no tea here.
5. I'm going to my friend's house with Negar.

**H4 — Conversion (3 min).** Rewrite H3 in colloquial form.

## Tutor notes

- Two systems in one lesson is the heaviest load of Unit 1 after L03. If time runs short, cut the هست/است distinction to a mention and give the hour to suffixes + prepositions — those are far more frequent.
- The ezâfe-preposition group is where L03 pays off. If the student drops the linking vowel here, it means L03 didn't fully land; re-drill it rather than moving on.
- Watch for double-marking (کتابِ من**م**). Common and worth catching early.
- The خونمون note is genuinely useful social information — don't skip it as flavor. Register errors around family words are noticeable.
- Next lesson is numbers again, which is a lighter cognitive load. Good place to catch up if this one overruns.
