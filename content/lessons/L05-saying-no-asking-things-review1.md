# Lesson 05 — Saying No & Asking Things

3. Translate: *Negar goes to work every day.*

## 1. Grammar A — Negation (10 min)

### 1a. The نـ prefix

Stick **نـ** (na‑) on the front of the verb. That's the whole rule.

> دارم → **ن**دارم — nadâram — *I don't have*
> رفتم → **ن**رفتم — naraftam — *I didn't go*

### 1b. Before می‌, it becomes نِمی‌

When there's a می‌ prefix, نـ goes in front of it and the vowel changes from **a** to **e**:

> می‌روم → **نمی‌**روم — **ne**miravam — *I don't go*
> می‌خورم → **نمی‌**خورم — nemikhoram — *I don't eat*
> می‌دانم → **نمی‌**دانم — nemidânam — *I don't know*

**نمی‌دانم** — nemidânam — is the single most useful sentence you will learn this month. Colloquially: **نمی‌دونم**.

### 1c. The stress moves

The negative prefix takes the stress, stealing it from می‌:

> **می‌**روم → **نِ**می‌روم — **NE**miravam

This is how a listener knows instantly whether you said yes or no, even at speed. Get it wrong and Persian speakers genuinely mishear you.

### 1d. Negating "to be"

بودن has its own negative stem, **نیست‑**:

| | Farsi | Translit |
|---|---|---|
| I am not | نیستم | nistam |
| you are not | نیستی | nisti |
| he/she is not | نیست | nist |
| we are not | نیستیم | nistim |
| you are not | نیستید | nistid |
| they are not | نیستند | nistand |

> من ایرانی نیستم. — *I am not Iranian.*
> این کتاب خوب نیست. — *This book is not good.*

Never نهستم.

## 2. Grammar B — Questions (10 min)

### 2a. Yes/no questions

There is **no word order change and no helper verb**. You just raise your intonation.

> شما ایرانی هستید. — *You are Iranian.*
> شما ایرانی هستید؟ — *Are you Iranian?*

Note the Persian question mark: **؟** — a mirrored version of ours.

In formal or written Persian you can put **آیا** (âyâ) at the front to signal a question explicitly:

> آیا شما ایرانی هستید؟

آیا sounds stiff in conversation. Recognize it in text; don't say it out loud.

### 2b. Question words

| Farsi | Translit | English |
|---|---|---|
| چه / چی | che / chi | what |
| کی | ki | who |
| کِی | key | when |
| کجا | kojâ | where |
| چرا | cherâ | why |
| چطور | chetor | how |
| چند | chand | how many |
| چقدر | cheqadr | how much |
| کدام | kodâm | which |

> **کی** (ki, "who") and **کِی** (key, "when") are spelled identically. Only context and the vowel separate them. Iranians disambiguate in speech by the vowel; in writing you infer it.

### 2c. The rule English speakers get wrong

**The question word stays where the answer would go.** Persian does not move it to the front.

> تو **به دانشگاه** می‌روی. → تو **کجا** می‌روی؟
> *You go to the university.* → *Where do you go?*
>
> او **چای** می‌خورد. → او **چی** می‌خورد؟
> *He drinks tea.* → *What does he drink?*

English yanks the question word to the front ("**Where** do you go?"). Persian leaves it in the slot. So the verb still ends the sentence:

> ✅ کجا می‌روی؟
> ❌ کجا تو می‌روی رفتن؟

**More patterns:**
> اسم شما چیست؟ — *What is your name?*
> این چیست؟ — *What is this?*
> کی می‌آید؟ — *Who is coming?*
> چرا نمی‌آیی؟ — *Why aren't you coming?*
> چطور هستید؟ — *How are you?*

### 2d. Answering

| Farsi | Translit | |
|---|---|---|
| بله | bale | yes *(neutral/formal)* |
| آره | âre | yeah *(casual)* |
| نه | na | no |
| خیر | kheyr | no *(formal)* |
| شاید | shâyad | maybe |
| البته | albatte | of course |
| حتماً | hatman | definitely |

**A freebie Persian has and English doesn't:** **چرا!** (cherâ) also means *"Yes it is!"* — used to contradict a negative question.

> — تو ایرانی نیستی؟ *(You're not Iranian?)*
> — **چرا**، هستم! *(Yes I am!)*

## 3. Guided practice (8 min)

**Drill A — Negate.** In script: می‌روم / دارد / هستیم / می‌خوانند / می‌نویسی / است.

**Drill B — Translate.**
1. I don't know.
2. She is not a teacher.
3. We don't drink tea.
4. They don't have a car.
5. This house is not big.

**Drill C — Make the question.** For each statement, ask about the underlined element.
1. من به **دانشگاه** می‌روم. *(→ where?)*
2. **نگار** چای می‌خورد. *(→ who?)*
3. او **کتاب** می‌خواند. *(→ what?)*
4. من **سه** کتاب دارم. *(→ how many?)*

**Drill D — Fix the error.**
1. من نه می‌روم.
2. او نهست ایرانی.
3. کجا تو می‌روی؟
4. من نمی‌دارم.

**R2 — Read aloud & translate.** No transliteration provided — this is the test.
> نگار دانشجو است. او هر روز به دانشگاه می‌رود. او یک ماشینِ کوچک دارد. ماشینش قدیمی است اما خیلی خوب است. نگار چای می‌خورد و کتاب می‌خواند. او آمریکایی نیست، ایرانی است.

- Six forms of بودن, positive and negative
- ‑ها vs ‑ان plurals; the number-kills-plural rule
- Producing an ezâfe chain out loud with the linking vowel audible
- Ten present stems, cold
- Verb-final word order under pressure
- نمی‑ with correct stress

## 5. How Negar Actually Says It

| Formal | Colloquial |
|---|---|
| نمی‌روم | نمی‌رم (nemiram) |
| نمی‌دانم | نمی‌دونم (nemidunam) |
| نیست | نیست / نیس (nis) |
| نمی‌خواهم | نمی‌خوام (nemikhâm) |
| بله | آره |
| این چیست؟ | این چیه؟ |
| کجا می‌روی؟ | کجا می‌ری؟ |
| کدام | کدوم |
| چقدر | چقد |
| اسم شما چیست؟ | اسمت چیه؟ / اسمتون چیه؟ |

**Phrases you'll hear ten times a day:**
- **نمی‌دونم** — I don't know
- **چی؟** — What? *(also "huh?")*
- **چرا که نه** — why not
- **مگه نه؟** — right? / isn't it? *(tag question — extremely common)*

## 6. Negar Drill 🗣️

**Time ask: 6 minutes.**

> **Part 1 — The yes/no test.** This is the important one. Say these six to Matt in random order at natural speed. He has to tell you whether each was positive or negative. If he can't hear the difference, he isn't putting stress on the نـ.
> ۱. می‌رم / ۲. نمی‌رم / ۳. داره / ۴. نداره / ۵. می‌خوام / ۶. نمی‌خوام
>
> **Part 2 — Interview him.** Ask these in your natural voice and make him answer in full Farsi sentences:
> اسمت چیه؟ · کجا کار می‌کنی؟ · چای می‌خوری؟ · ماشین داری؟ · هر روز چیکار می‌کنی؟ · ایرانی هستی؟
>
> **Part 3 —** Ask him one question he *can't* answer yet, and let him say **نمی‌دونم** or **بلد نیستم** ("I don't know how"). Getting comfortable saying that is more useful than any grammar point.

## 7. Homework (20 min)

**H1 — Negation drill (5 min).** Take your ten verbs from L04. Write the 1sg and 3sg of each in the negative, script. Then the colloquial version of each.

**H2 — Twenty questions (6 min).** Write ten questions you'd actually want to ask Negar, using each question word at least once. Real questions, not exercises — you're going to ask them.

**H3 — Interview (5 min).** Ask Negar your ten questions. Write her answers in script as best you can hear them. Bring anything you couldn't catch to the next lesson.

**H4 — Correction pass (4 min).** Go back over Review R1. For each sentence you got wrong, write the correct version three times and note *which rule* you broke — not just the right answer.

## Tutor notes

- **Gate here.** Review 1 is a real checkpoint. If R1 scores below 7/10, spend the next session re-drilling rather than starting L06. Numbers (L06) can wait; a broken foundation can't.
- Stress placement on نمی‌ is worth more attention than it seems. It's the difference between being understood and being asked to repeat yourself for the next year.
- Watch for fronted question words — it's the most persistent L1 interference error at this stage.
- R2 contains ماشینش, a possessive suffix from L08, on purpose. Note whether the student can infer it from context; that's a good early signal of reading instinct.
- If the student is ahead of pace, don't accelerate — deepen. Add vocabulary and more Negar time rather than pulling L06 forward.
